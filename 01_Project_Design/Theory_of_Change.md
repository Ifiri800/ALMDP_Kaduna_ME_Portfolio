# Theory of Change
## Agricultural Livelihoods and MSME Development Project (ALMDP)

**Project Status:** Synthetic/Simulated Portfolio Project  
**Geographic Focus:** Kaduna State, Nigeria  
**Implementation Period:** January 2027 – December 2030  
**Target Beneficiaries:** 10,000 direct beneficiaries  
**Primary Beneficiaries:** 8,000 smallholder farmers and 2,000 agricultural MSMEs  
**Value Chains:** Maize, rice, soybean, ginger, tomato, vegetables, sorghum and groundnut

> **Portfolio Disclaimer:** This is a synthetic Monitoring & Evaluation portfolio project developed for demonstration of M&E planning, data management, analysis, reporting and dashboard capabilities. It does not represent an actual donor-funded project, actual intervention, or verified Kaduna State programme data.

---

## 1. Purpose

The Theory of Change explains how the planned resources and interventions of the Agricultural Livelihoods and MSME Development Project (ALMDP) are expected to contribute to improved agricultural productivity, market participation, agricultural MSME performance, inclusion and economic resilience.

It provides the causal logic connecting project inputs and activities to outputs, short-term outcomes, medium-term outcomes and the intended long-term impact.

The Theory of Change will guide the project's results framework, indicator system, data collection instruments, monitoring activities, baseline assessment, analysis, reporting and learning processes.

---

## 2. Development Problem

Smallholder farmers and agricultural MSMEs face interconnected constraints that can limit productivity, income generation and economic resilience.

Key constraints addressed by the project include:

- Limited adoption of improved agricultural practices and technologies.
- Inadequate access to agricultural extension and technical knowledge.
- Limited access to reliable market information.
- Weak connections between producers, buyers and structured markets.
- High post-harvest losses.
- Weak business management and financial record-keeping among agricultural MSMEs.
- Limited participation and economic opportunities for women and young people.
- Exposure to agricultural, economic and climate-related shocks.
- Limited availability of reliable monitoring information for management decision-making.

These constraints are interconnected. Limited technical knowledge can reduce adoption of improved practices; low adoption can constrain productivity; weak market access can reduce selling opportunities; poor post-harvest management can reduce the quantity and value of marketable production; and weak business management can constrain MSME profitability and resilience.

The project therefore applies an integrated intervention model rather than addressing agricultural production, markets, businesses and inclusion independently.

---

## 3. Project Goal

The overall goal of ALMDP is:

> **To improve agricultural productivity, market participation, agricultural MSME performance and economic resilience among smallholder farmers and agricultural MSMEs in selected LGAs of Kaduna State.**

---

## 4. Theory of Change Statement

If ALMDP provides farmers and agricultural MSMEs with relevant technical training, climate-smart agricultural support, demonstration opportunities, market information, structured market linkages and business development support, while maintaining an effective monitoring and learning system, then beneficiaries are expected to improve their knowledge, adoption of recommended practices, market access, post-harvest management and business management practices.

These improvements are expected to contribute to higher agricultural productivity, reduced post-harvest losses, improved MSME revenue and profitability, increased market participation and increased beneficiary income.

Over time, these changes are expected to contribute to improved livelihoods and greater economic resilience among participating farmers and agricultural MSMEs.

The causal pathway is:

**Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Short-Term Outcomes → Medium-Term Outcomes → Impact**

# 7. Short-Term Outcomes

The project expects the following short-term changes among participating beneficiaries.

## 7.1 Improved Agricultural Knowledge

Training, demonstration plots and technical support are expected to increase beneficiaries' understanding of recommended agricultural practices.

**Key indicator:**

- OUT-03: Percentage of trained farmers demonstrating adequate knowledge.

Adequate knowledge will be measured using a structured knowledge assessment, with a defined threshold for satisfactory performance.

---

## 7.2 Increased Adoption of Recommended Agricultural Practices

Improved knowledge and practical exposure are expected to increase the adoption of recommended agricultural practices.

**Key indicator:**

- OUT-02: Percentage of farmers adopting at least three recommended practices.

Adoption will be assessed using the agricultural practices module of the monitoring instrument.

---

## 7.3 Improved Buyer and Market Access

Market information and market-linkage activities are expected to improve beneficiaries' ability to identify and access buyers.

**Key indicators:**

- OUT-05: Percentage of beneficiaries reporting improved buyer access.
- OUT-04: Percentage of farmers selling through structured markets.

---

## 7.4 Improved Post-Harvest Management

Training and improved access to storage and handling practices are expected to reduce post-harvest losses.

**Key indicator:**

- OUT-06: Average post-harvest loss rate.

Because lower losses represent better performance, this indicator is an inverse indicator.

---

## 7.5 Improved MSME Financial Management

Business development and financial management training are expected to improve the ability of agricultural MSMEs to maintain basic financial records and manage business operations.

**Key indicator:**

- OUT-09: Percentage of supported MSMEs maintaining basic financial records.

---

## 7.6 Increased Participation of Women and Youth

The project will deliberately monitor participation and outcomes among women and young people to ensure that project benefits are inclusive.

**Key indicators:**

- OUT-10: Percentage of direct beneficiaries who are women.
- OUT-11: Percentage of direct beneficiaries aged 18–35.
- OUT-12: Percentage of women beneficiaries reporting increased agricultural/business income.

# 8. Medium-Term Outcomes

The short-term outcomes are expected to contribute to broader changes in agricultural and business performance.

## 8.1 Increased Crop Productivity

Increased adoption of improved practices is expected to improve agricultural productivity.

**Key indicator:**

- OUT-01: Average crop productivity measured in tonnes per hectare.

Productivity will be calculated as:

**Crop productivity = Quantity harvested (tonnes) / Area cultivated (hectares)**

---

## 8.2 Reduced Post-Harvest Losses

Improved production handling, storage and post-harvest practices are expected to reduce the proportion of harvested production lost.

**Key indicator:**

- OUT-06: Average post-harvest loss rate.

The post-harvest loss rate will be calculated as:

**Post-harvest loss rate = Quantity lost / Quantity harvested × 100**

---

## 8.3 Improved Agricultural MSME Performance

Improved business practices, financial management and market access are expected to improve agricultural MSME performance.

**Key indicators:**

- OUT-07: Percentage of supported MSMEs reporting increased annual revenue.
- OUT-08: Average MSME annual profit.
- OUT-09: Percentage of supported MSMEs maintaining basic financial records.

---

## 8.4 Increased Market Participation and Beneficiary Income

Improved buyer access, structured market participation, increased productivity and reduced losses are expected to contribute to improved economic returns.

**Key indicators:**

- OUT-04: Percentage of farmers selling through structured markets.
- OUT-05: Percentage of beneficiaries reporting improved buyer access.
- IMP-01: Average annual beneficiary income.

The project expects improved productivity, stronger market participation, better business performance, increased income and improved capacity to cope with shocks to contribute collectively to improved livelihood conditions and economic resilience.

**Key impact indicators:**

- IMP-01: Average annual beneficiary income.

The project will assess these indicators periodically to determine whether the observed changes are consistent with the expected results pathway.

# 15. Contribution Rather Than Attribution

The project will use a contribution-based interpretation of results.

Changes in beneficiary income, productivity, market participation or resilience may be influenced by factors outside the project, including weather, market prices, government programmes, input availability, household characteristics and broader economic conditions.

Therefore, observed changes will not automatically be attributed entirely to ALMDP.

The M&E system will instead assess whether observed changes are consistent with the project's Theory of Change and whether project activities and outputs provide credible evidence of contribution.

This will be supported through:

- Baseline measurements.
- Periodic monitoring.
- Outcome measurements.
- Beneficiary feedback.
- Comparison of performance against targets.
- Documentation of implementation intensity.
- Analysis of contextual factors.
- Qualitative explanations of observed changes.
- Triangulation of quantitative and qualitative evidence.

---

# 16. Learning and Adaptive Management

Monitoring information will be used not only for reporting but also for management and learning.

The adaptive management cycle will be:

**Measure → Analyze → Identify Variance → Investigate Cause → Decide → Act → Monitor → Learn**

Where performance falls below target, management will:

1. Identify the variance.
2. Determine the likely cause.
3. Review supporting evidence.
4. Identify corrective actions.
5. Assign responsibility.
6. Establish a completion deadline.
7. Monitor implementation.
8. Assess whether performance improves.

Lessons from implementation will be documented and used to refine activities and management decisions.

# 18. Management Use of M&E Evidence

The M&E system will provide evidence for management decisions at multiple levels.

### Implementation management

Monitoring data will identify whether planned activities and outputs are being delivered on schedule.

### Performance management

Outcome indicators will show whether implementation is producing the expected changes among beneficiaries.

### Resource management

Performance information will support decisions about where additional technical support, resources or management attention may be required.

### Risk management

Monitoring information will help identify emerging implementation, market, environmental and data-quality risks.

### Learning

Outcome and qualitative evidence will help identify which interventions appear to be working, where performance is weaker and what implementation adjustments may be required.

**Resources and technical capacity**

↓

**Training, demonstrations, market linkages, MSME support and M&E activities**

↓

**Improved delivery of project services**

↓

**Farmers trained, MSMEs supported, market linkages established and reliable M&E information generated**

↓

↓

**Higher productivity, lower post-harvest losses, stronger MSME performance, greater market participation and increased income**
