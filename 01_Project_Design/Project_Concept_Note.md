# Agricultural Livelihoods and MSME Development Project (ALMDP)

## Project Concept Note

**Project Status:** Synthetic/Simulated Portfolio Project  
**Location:** Kaduna State, Nigeria  
**Implementation Period:** January 2027 – December 2030  
**Project Duration:** 4 years  
**Target Beneficiaries:** 10,000 direct beneficiaries  
**Primary Beneficiaries:** 8,000 smallholder farmers and 2,000 agricultural MSMEs

---

## 1. Project Background

The Agricultural Livelihoods and MSME Development Project (ALMDP) is a simulated donor-funded agricultural and rural economic development project designed for professional Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) portfolio development.

The project is designed around the need to improve agricultural productivity, market participation, agricultural MSME performance, income generation, and economic resilience among smallholder farmers and agricultural enterprises in selected Local Government Areas (LGAs) of Kaduna State, Nigeria.

The project provides a practical setting for demonstrating the design and implementation of a complete M&E system, including results-based management, indicator development, baseline assessment, data collection, data quality assurance, quantitative analysis, visualization, reporting, learning, and management decision-making.

**Important portfolio designation:** This project is entirely synthetic. The project, beneficiary figures, baseline values, targets, survey observations, and analytical results will be generated for demonstration purposes and must not be presented as actual donor-funded project data.

The geographic structure will allow M&E analysis at both project level and LGA level.

## 3. Target Beneficiaries

The project will target 10,000 direct beneficiaries:

- 8,000 smallholder farmers.
- 2,000 agricultural MSMEs.

The project will prioritize women, youth, and other potentially underserved beneficiary groups while maintaining systematic beneficiary registration and disaggregation by sex, age, LGA, beneficiary type, and agricultural value chain.

---

## 4. Agricultural Value Chains

The principal agricultural value chains covered by the project will include:

- Tomato

The value-chain structure will support analysis of differences in productivity, market access, post-harvest losses, adoption of improved practices, and beneficiary income.

---

## 5. Overall Goal

The overall goal of ALMDP will be to improve the livelihoods and economic resilience of smallholder farmers and agricultural MSMEs in selected LGAs of Kaduna State.

---

## 6. Project Objectives

The project will pursue four primary objectives:

### Objective 1: Agricultural Productivity

Increase the adoption of improved agricultural practices and climate-smart technologies among targeted smallholder farmers.

### Objective 2: Market Access

Improve market participation and access to reliable buyers and market information among targeted farmers.

### Objective 3: MSME Development

Improve the business performance, financial management practices, and profitability of agricultural MSMEs.

### Objective 4: Inclusion and Resilience

Increase the participation of women and youth and strengthen the economic resilience of project beneficiaries.

## 8. Theory of Change

The project will use a results-based Theory of Change linking resources and implementation activities to measurable outputs, outcomes, and impact.

### Inputs

Project financing, technical personnel, partnerships, training resources, M&E systems, data collection tools, agricultural knowledge, market relationships, and institutional support.

### Activities

Farmer training, demonstration plots, climate-smart agriculture support, market-linkage activities, market information dissemination, MSME business support, financial management training, data collection, monitoring, verification, and reporting.

### Outputs

Farmers trained, farmers completing climate-smart agriculture training, demonstration plots established, market-linkage events conducted, farmers receiving market information, MSMEs supported, MSMEs completing financial management training, and M&E activities and reports completed.

### Short-Term Outcomes

Improved agricultural knowledge, increased adoption of recommended practices, improved business management practices, increased access to market information, improved buyer access, and improved MSME financial record keeping.

### Medium-Term Outcomes

Improved agricultural productivity, increased structured-market participation, reduced post-harvest losses, increased MSME revenue and profitability, increased beneficiary income, and improved economic resilience.

### Impact

Improved livelihoods and economic resilience among targeted smallholder farmers and agricultural MSMEs.

---

## 9. Key Assumptions

The project Theory of Change will rely on several assumptions:

1. Target beneficiaries will participate in project activities.
2. Agricultural inputs and services will remain reasonably accessible.
3. Farmers and MSMEs will be willing to adopt promoted practices.
4. Demand for agricultural products will remain sufficient to support market participation.
5. Market actors will participate in linkage activities.
6. Climate and other external shocks will remain manageable within project adaptation capacity.
7. Project financing will be available according to the implementation schedule.
8. Beneficiary and implementation data will be collected accurately and submitted on time.
9. Project management will use M&E evidence to support corrective action and adaptive management.

---

## 10. Monitoring and Evaluation Approach

The ALMDP M&E system will follow a results-based management approach.

The M&E architecture will connect:

**Results Framework → Indicators → Indicator Definitions → Data Requirements → Data Collection Tools → Data Dictionary → Dataset → Data Quality Assurance → Indicator Calculation → Dashboard → Reporting → Management Decision**

The M&E system will combine quantitative beneficiary data, administrative records, activity monitoring data, field verification, and qualitative implementation evidence.

---

## 11. Baseline Assessment

A synthetic baseline survey will be developed to establish starting values for the project's outcome and impact indicators.

The simulated baseline dataset will contain 2,000 respondent records representing farmers and agricultural MSMEs across the seven selected LGAs.

The baseline assessment will collect information on:

- Beneficiary characteristics.
- Agricultural production.
- Agricultural practices.
- Market access.
- Post-harvest losses.
- Agricultural and business income.
- MSME performance.
- Financial record keeping.
- Economic resilience.
- Agricultural knowledge.

The baseline dataset will intentionally contain controlled data-quality issues for demonstration of the project's data-quality assurance and data-cleaning processes.

---

## 12. Data Quality Management

The M&E system will assess data using five core quality dimensions:

- Accuracy
- Completeness
- Consistency
- Timeliness
- Integrity

Automated validation rules, manual review, source verification, duplicate detection, logical consistency checks, range checks, and periodic Data Quality Assessments (DQAs) will be used to protect the integrity of reported results.

---

## 13. Reporting and Visualization

The M&E system will produce:

- Monthly monitoring reports.
- Quarterly performance reports.
- Annual performance reports.
- Baseline report.
- Indicator performance tables.
- Data quality reports.
- M&E dashboards.
- Lessons-learned products.
- Management decision briefs.

Dashboard reporting will allow project management to examine performance by indicator, LGA, sex, age group, beneficiary type, and agricultural value chain.

---

## 14. Learning and Adaptive Management

M&E findings will be used not only for accountability but also for learning and project improvement.

Performance gaps will be investigated to identify implementation constraints, document lessons, recommend corrective actions, and monitor whether corrective measures improve subsequent performance.

The project will therefore use an iterative cycle:

**Measure → Analyze → Interpret → Decide → Act → Monitor**

## 17. Project Documentation Principle

All project documentation will maintain a clear audit trail from the project objective through to reported results.

The core documentation chain will be:

**Objective → Result → Indicator → Definition → Data Source → Collection Tool → Variable → Validation Rule → Clean Data → Calculation → Dashboard → Report → Management Action**

This traceability will ensure that every reported indicator can be linked back to its underlying data source and measurement methodology.
